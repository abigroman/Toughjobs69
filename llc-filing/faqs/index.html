Pricing & Costs

What does the price actually include?

There's no service charge — you only pay the state directly. Illinois' Articles of Organization fee is $150 plus a mandatory ~$3.38 online processing fee; Iowa's Certificate of Organization fee is $50. Optional add-ons like 24-hour Illinois expedited processing (+$100) or a name reservation are separate state fees, priced only if you choose them.

How much does it cost to form an LLC in Illinois?

The Illinois state filing fee for Articles of Organization is $150, plus a mandatory ~$3.38 online processing fee if you file through the online portal. Toughjobs files it for free — we don't add a service charge. Optional 24-hour expedited processing is +$100 (standard processing takes 10–14 days), and reserving your name before filing is $25 for 90 days. Forming a Series LLC instead costs $400.

How much does it cost to form an LLC in Iowa?

The Iowa state filing fee for a Certificate of Organization is $50 — one of the lowest in the country — and Toughjobs files it for you at no charge. Optional extras are separate state fees: a name reservation is $10, filing a fictitious name (DBA) is $5, and registering a foreign LLC already formed in another state is $100.

Are there ongoing fees after I file?

Yes. Illinois requires a $75 Annual Report, due every year before the first day of your LLC's anniversary month — more than 60 days late and Illinois adds a $100 late penalty. Iowa requires a Biennial Report every odd-numbered year between January 1 and April 1, for $30 online or $45 by mail. These are separate, recurring state fees — not part of your initial filing total.

Why do prices say "starting"?

State fees are set by the state and can change at any time. We always confirm the current, final price with you before anything is filed, so there are no surprises.

Timeline & Completion

How long does it take?

Timelines are set by the state, not by us. Iowa online filings are often approved within a business day or two; Illinois standard processing takes longer, and expedited is faster. We'll give you the current estimate when you start.

Requirements & Preparation

What's a registered agent, and do I need one?

A registered agent is a person or company with a physical address in your state who receives official mail for your LLC. Both Illinois and Iowa require one. In Iowa, acting as your own agent (or having a partner do it) is free; a professional registered agent service generally runs $100–$300/year. You can often be your own agent, or we can help you coordinate a service as an add-on.

What information and documents do you need from me?

For Illinois: your desired LLC name, principal business address in Illinois, a registered agent and Illinois address, member/manager names, and standard vs. expedited processing. For Iowa: desired LLC name, principal mailing address, a registered agent and physical Iowa address, member/manager names, and any desired effective date.

Do I need to choose a business name before starting?

Yes — we check your desired name against the state's records as part of the filing prep, before submission.

What business address and ownership information will be required?

Your principal business address, a registered agent with a physical address in the filing state, and the names of all members/managers.

Licensing & Compliance

What is the difference between forming an LLC and obtaining a business license?

An LLC filing registers your business entity with the state. Trade licenses, local permits, and professional licensing are separate requirements that are your responsibility — forming an LLC doesn't cover them.

Does Toughjobs provide legal, tax, or accounting advice?

No. We don't advise whether an LLC is right for you, your tax classification, or ownership splits — that's a decision for you and a licensed attorney or CPA.

Should I consult an attorney or tax professional before choosing a business structure?

Yes, especially for anything beyond a simple, straightforward LLC — multiple owners, complex agreements, unusual tax questions, or legal risk are all reasons to consult a licensed attorney or CPA, and we'll be honest if that's the better path.
