The rundown

Business Cards and Stationery built around a specific job.

Business cards for contractors should not begin with a generic layout. The design decision starts before colors and type. Define whether this is for a service call, estimate, referral conversation, or office handoff, then choose the message and format that can survive the real viewing conditions. A useful piece gives the customer enough context to take the next step without asking print to explain the whole company.

Before design, Toughjobs documents who needs a card, the contact route, role labels, writable space, and whether matching stationery is needed. The first proof is checked as a working communication tool: Can the intended reader understand it in the available time? Is the main action obvious? Does every claim match what the business can actually deliver?

Straight answers

Business Cards & Stationery FAQ

What should we provide for a business cards and stationery quote?

Provide the intended use, approximate quantity, deadline, delivery location, available artwork, and any known production specifications. Important planning details include who needs a card, the contact route, role labels, writable space, and whether matching stationery is needed.

Does Toughjobs handle both design and production for business cards and stationery?

The scope can include message planning, copy, design, proof preparation, and production coordination. The quote should state which services, materials, shipping, mailing, installation, or vendor costs are included.

What should be checked before the piece goes to production?

Review spelling, phone numbers, URLs, QR destinations, offer terms, and brand details. The production check should also confirm names, titles, phone numbers, email, QR destination, stock, finish, and quantity.
