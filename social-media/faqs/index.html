Pricing & Costs

How much does social media management cost?

Basic Presence runs $400–$800/mo (2 platforms, 8–12 posts/mo from photos you send). Standard Management runs $900–$1,800/mo (2–3 platforms, 16–20 posts/mo, story/reel production, review response). Content + Video Program runs $2,000–$4,000/mo (monthly on-site shoot, edited reels/shorts). A Paid Social Layer adds $500–$3,000/mo ad spend plus $500/mo management. Full Social Program (everything combined) runs $3,500–$7,000/mo all-in.

What factors determine the monthly price?

Number of platforms, posting volume, whether content comes from photos you send versus an on-site shoot we run, and whether paid social is layered in.

Is content creation included in the management fee?

Yes at every tier, scaled to the plan — Basic Presence works from photos you send, while the Content + Video Program includes a monthly on-site shoot and edited reels/shorts.

Are paid social advertising and advertising spend included?

Not in the base management tiers — paid social is a separate layer ($500–$3,000/mo ad spend plus $500/mo management), or bundled into the all-in Full Social Program.

Does managing additional platforms increase the price?

Yes — Basic Presence covers 2 platforms, Standard Management covers 2–3, and pricing scales with platform count and posting volume.

Getting Started

How do I get started with social media management?

Talk to us about your market — no pitch, just a straight read on what would actually move the needle for your business.

Which social media platforms should my business use?

It depends on what you're solving for. Google Business Profile has the highest direct ranking impact, Reddit ranks well for high-intent "near me" threads, Facebook drives local reach and reviews, YouTube works for how-to and project videos, Instagram/TikTok build brand demand with before/after visuals, and Nextdoor drives hyper-local neighborhood trust. You don't need to be everywhere — just consistent where your customers already are.

Our Process

How often should I post?

3–5 times per week per platform is the sweet spot. Below 2/week and the algorithm stops showing you to your own followers. Above 7/week is usually a waste unless you're running a heavy content operation. Consistency matters more than volume — a steady 3/week beats a burst of 15 followed by silence.

How do you develop the content strategy and calendar?

Four moves: set up and optimize every platform that matters for your trade and town, post real job photos and before/afters on a steady schedule, engage helpfully in community spaces like Nextdoor and local groups, and run systematic review prompts.

How do you handle timely posts, trends, and unexpected events?

Community engagement is built into the process — helpful answers in local groups and Nextdoor threads, not scheduled posts on autopilot.

Customization & Options

Do I really need to be on TikTok?

No. Most contractors get 90% of their social ROI from Facebook and Instagram, with Nextdoor as the sleeper. TikTok and Shorts are worth it if you (or a crew member) are naturally comfortable on camera — otherwise the effort-to-return ratio doesn't pencil out for a busy contractor.

Should I boost posts or run real ads?

Real ads, through Meta Business Manager, almost every time. Boost buttons are Meta's way of taking your money for lower-quality placements and worse targeting. A properly built lead-form campaign will outperform boosted posts by 3–5x on cost per lead.

Can you manage Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, or other platforms?

Yes, matched to what actually moves the needle for your trade — Facebook and Instagram for local reach and visuals, YouTube for how-to content, TikTok for emerging reach, Nextdoor for neighborhood trust, and Google Business Profile and Reddit for direct search impact.

Can you manage contests, promotions, launches, events, or influencer collaborations?

Seasonal promotions and lead-form campaigns are part of the Paid Social Layer; broader campaign work is scoped per project.

Can social media be coordinated with SEO, paid ads, and reputation management?

Yes — social activity directly feeds review volume, brand searches, and backlinks, all of which are ranking signals, and the Paid Social Layer coordinates with organic posting rather than running separately.

Results & Expectations

Does social media help SEO?

Indirectly, and significantly. Social drives branded searches (people Googling your name after seeing your post), referral traffic to your site, and — for Google Business Profile — signals of an active, real business. It also fuels reviews, which are a direct local ranking factor. Social isn't an SEO tactic, but it feeds the machine.

What is more important: followers, engagement, reach, traffic, or conversions?

Leads generated, not just "reach." Reporting covers engagement and follower growth AND leads generated — vanity numbers alone don't tell you if it's working.

How do organic social media and paid social advertising work together?

Organic builds the brand searches, reviews, and community trust that make paid ads convert better; the Paid Social Layer (lead ads, retargeting, seasonal promotions) is optimized weekly on top of that organic foundation.

Support & Maintenance

What do I do about negative comments or reviews?

Respond publicly, quickly, and professionally — always. Acknowledge, apologize where warranted, offer to take it offline. How you handle criticism in public is one of the most-read signals of your business's character, and a well-handled negative review often converts better than five glowing ones.
